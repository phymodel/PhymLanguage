# Spaceflight

## Formula

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Kepler's third law: } &\frac{a^3}{T^2}=k \\
\text{Law of universal gravitation: } &F=G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

## Example 1

(**2024 National Paper A, Q 2-16**) (6 points) In May 2024 the Chang'e-6 probe was launched successfully, beginning the first journey in human history to sample and return from the far side of the Moon. To bring the samples collected back to the Earth, the spacecraft has to go through such processes as lift-off from the lunar surface, flight around the Moon, and transfer from the Moon to the Earth. The free-fall acceleration at the surface of the Moon is about $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of that at the surface of the Earth. Which of the following statements is correct? ($\qquad$)
A. During the flight around the Moon, the net force on the sample is zero
B. If the sample is placed on the near side of the Moon, its pressure on the lunar surface equals zero
C. The gravitational force on the sample differs in different processes, so its mass differs too
D. When the sample is placed on the far side of the Moon, its pressure on the Moon is smaller than its pressure on the Earth when placed on the surface of the Earth
**Analysis:**
(1) The net force on the sample is the gravitational force; A is wrong.
(2) By Newton's third law, its pressure on the lunar surface equals the gravity acting on it; B is wrong.
(3) From $F=G\dfrac{m_1m_2}{r^2}$, different $r$ gives different $F$, but the mass is unchanged; C is wrong.
(4) By Newton's third law and Newton's second law, $F_\text{press}=G=mg$, and $g_\text{Earth}>g_\text{Moon}$, so the pressure of the sample on the Moon when placed on the far side is smaller than its pressure on the Earth when placed on the surface of the Earth; D is correct.
**Answer:** $\rm D$.
**Formulas:** Newton's second law $F=ma$; law of universal gravitation $F=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$.

## Example 2

(**2024 National New Curriculum Paper, Q 2-16**) (6 points) Astronomers have found that a red dwarf outside the solar system has two planets orbiting it, among which the orbit of the planet $\rm GJ1002c$ is approximately a circle, with an orbital radius about $0.07$ times the Earth–Sun distance and a period of about $0.06$ year. Then the mass of this red dwarf is about ($\qquad$) the mass of the Sun.

| A. $0.001$ times	| B. $0.1$ times | C. $10$ times | D. $4$ times |
|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|

**Analysis:** The centripetal force is provided by the gravitational force, so
$$
\frac{GMm}{r^2}=m\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^2r,
$$
which gives
$$
M=\frac{4\pi^2r^3}{GT^2};
$$
then the mass of the red dwarf is
$$
M_1=\frac{4\pi^2r_1^3}{GT_1^2},
$$
and the mass of the Sun is
$$
M_2=\frac{4\pi^2r_2^3}{GT_2^2};
$$
therefore
$$
\frac{M_1}{M_2}=\frac{r_1^3}{r_2^3}\cdot\frac{T_2^2}{T_1^2}=(0.07)^3\cdot\frac{1}{(0.06)^2}\approx 0.1.
$$
Hence B is correct.
**Answer:** B.
**Formulas:** centripetal force $F_n=m\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\right)^2r$; law of universal gravitation $F=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$.

## Example 3

(**2022 National Paper B, Q 2-14**) (6 points) In March 2022 the Chinese astronauts Zhai Zhigang, Wang Yaping and Ye Guangfu, on board the "Tiangong-2" space station about $400\,\rm km$ above the surface of the Earth, gave the students a wonderful science lesson through a space–ground live link. From the live broadcast it can be seen that in "Tiangong-2", which flies in a low-Earth circular orbit, the astronauts can float freely, which shows that they ($\qquad$).
A. experience a gravitational force from the Earth whose magnitude is approximately zero
B. experience a gravitational force from the Earth and a force from the spacecraft whose resultant is approximately zero
C. experience a gravitational force from the Earth whose magnitude approximately equals the centripetal force needed for their motion with the spacecraft
D. experience a gravitational force on the surface of the Earth whose magnitude is smaller than the centripetal force needed for their motion with the spacecraft
**Analysis:**
(1) By the law of universal gravitation $F=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$, the gravitational force on the astronauts is not zero. $\rm A$ is wrong.
(2) The gravitational force of the Earth on the astronauts exactly provides the centripetal force for their circular motion, and there is no other force. $\rm B$ is wrong, $\rm C$ is correct.
(3) Because $r_\text{surface}<r_\text{orbit}$, we have $F_\text{surface}>F_\text{orbit}$. $\rm D$ is wrong.
**Answer:** $\rm C$.
**Formula:** law of universal gravitation $F=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$

## Example 4

(**2023 National New Curriculum Paper, Q 2-17**) (6 points) In May 2023 the Tianzhou-6 cargo spacecraft, the one with the largest carrying capacity in service in the world, took about $5\, 800\,\rm kg$ of supplies into an orbit about $400\,\rm kg$ above the ground (smaller than the distance between a geostationary satellite and the ground), and after successfully docking with the Chinese space station it moves approximately in uniform circular motion. After docking, these supplies ($\qquad$).
A. have a smaller mass than when at rest on the ground
B. experience a smaller net force than when at rest on the ground
C. experience a greater gravitational force from the Earth than when at rest on the ground
D. move in circular motion with a greater angular speed than the angular speed of the Earth's rotation
**Analysis:**
(1) Mass is the amount of matter a body contains and is the physical quantity describing its inertia; mass does not depend on the state of motion. A is wrong.
(2) By the centripetal force formula $F_n=m\omega^2r$, since after docking the angular speed at the position of the supplies is greater than that at the ground and the orbital radius is greater than the radius of the Earth, the net force on the supplies after docking is greater than when they are at rest on the ground. B is wrong.
(3) By the law of universal gravitation $F=G\frac{Mm}{r^2}$, since after docking the orbital radius of the supplies is greater than the radius of the Earth, the gravitational force of the Earth on the supplies after docking is smaller than when they are at rest on the ground. C is wrong.
(4) Since the gravitational force provides the centripetal force, $G\frac{Mm}{r^2}=m\omega^2r$, we have $\omega=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r^3}}$; since after docking the orbital radius of the supplies is smaller than the orbital radius of a geostationary satellite, the angular speed of the supplies after docking is greater than that of a geostationary satellite, and the angular speed of a geostationary satellite equals the angular speed of the Earth, so after docking the angular speed of the supplies in circular motion is greater than the angular speed of the Earth's rotation. D is correct.
**Answer:** D.
**Formulas:** centripetal force $F_n=m\omega^2r$; law of universal gravitation $F=G\frac{Mm}{r^2}$.

## Example 5

(**2015 Jiangsu Province, Q 1-3**) (3 points) For the past thousands of years, human knowledge and study of the planets had been confined to the solar system; the discovery of the planet "$51\,\rm peg\, b$" opened the prelude to the study of planets outside the solar system. "$51\,\rm peg\, b$" moves in uniform circular motion around its central star, with a period of about  days and an orbital radius of about  that of the Earth around the Sun. The ratio of the mass of this central star to the mass of the Sun is about ($\qquad$).

| A. $\dfrac{1}{10}$ | B. $1$ | C. $5$ | D. $10$ |
|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|

**Analysis:**
The gravitational force provides the centripetal force, so the equations are
$$
\begin{cases}
G\dfrac{M_\text{Sun}m_\text{Earth}}{R^2}
=m_\text{Earth}\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{T_1}\right)^2R,\\
T_1=365\,\text{days},\\
G\dfrac{M_\text{star}m_b}{\left(\dfrac{R}{20}\right)^2}
=m_b\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{T_2}\right)^2\frac{R}{20},\\
T_2=4\,\text{days}.
\end{cases}
$$
Solving gives
$$
\frac{M_\text{star}}{M_\text{Sun}}=1.
$$
**Answer:** .
**Formulas:** centripetal force $F_n=m\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\right)^2r$; law of universal gravitation $F=G\dfrac{m_1m_2}{r^2}$.
