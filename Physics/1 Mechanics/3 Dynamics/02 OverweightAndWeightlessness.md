# Overweight and Weightlessness

## Formula

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Weightlessnes: } &F_{\rm N}=m(g-a)< mg \\
\text{Overweight: } &F_{\rm N}=m(g+a)> mg
\end{aligned}
}
$$

## Overweight and Weightlessness

Weightlessness: the phenomenon in which the pressure a body exerts on its support (or the pull it exerts on the object suspending it) is less than the gravity acting on the body is called weightlessness, that is, $F_{\rm N}=m(g-a)< mg$.
Overweight: the phenomenon in which the pressure a body exerts on its support (or the pull it exerts on the object suspending it) is greater than the gravity acting on the body is called overweight, that is, $F_{\rm N}=m(g+a)> mg$.
Complete weightlessness: the phenomenon in which the pressure a body exerts on its support (or the pull it exerts on the object suspending it) is zero and the downward vertical acceleration of the body is $a=g$ is called complete weightlessness, that is, $F_{\rm N}=m(g-g)< mg$.

## Example 1

(**2024 National Paper A, Q 3-22**) (5 points) To investigate the phenomena of overweight and weightlessness, a student group hangs a spring dynamometer inside an elevator and hangs an object on the lower end of the dynamometer. The magnitude of the local gravitational acceleration is known to be $9.8\,\rm m/s^2$.

![example1.html](./02 example1.html)

(1) When the elevator is at rest the reading of the dynamometer is as shown in the figure, and the reading is $\underline{\qquad\qquad}\, \rm N$ (keep $1$ decimal place);
(2) While the elevator moves upward, the reading of the dynamometer is $4.5\,\rm N$ for a period of time; then during this period the object is in the state of $\underline{\qquad\qquad}$ (fill in "overweight" or "weightless"), and the magnitude of the acceleration of the elevator is $\underline{\qquad\qquad}\,\rm m/s^2$ (keep $1$ decimal place).
**Analysis:**
(1) From the figure, the reading of the spring dynamometer is $5\,\rm N$;
(2) because $\rm 5.0 \,N>4.5\,\rm N$, the supporting force $F_N$ on the object inside the elevator is less than the gravity $mg$, so the object is in the state of weightlessness; by Newton's second law $mg-F_{\rm N}=ma$, substituting the data gives $a=1.0\,\rm m/s^2$.
**Answer:** (1) $5.0$; (2) weightless, $1.0$.
**Formulas:** net-force–acceleration relation $F=ma$; weightlessness $F_{\rm N}=m(g-a)< mg$.

## Example 2

(2015 Jiangsu Province, Q 2-6) (4 points) A person rides an elevator up, and during the vertical upward motion the graph of the acceleration $a$ against time $t$ is as shown in the figure, with the vertically upward direction taken as positive for $a$. Then the pressure of the person on the floor ($\qquad$).

![example2.html](./02 example2.html)

A. greatest at $t=2\,\rm s$
B. smallest at $t=2\,\rm s$
C. greatest at $t=8.5\,\rm s$
D. smallest at $t=8.5\,\rm s$
**Analysis:**
When $t=2\,\rm s$ the upward acceleration of the elevator is greatest, and at this moment the pressure of the person on the floor is greatest;
when $t=8.5\,\rm s$ the downward acceleration of the elevator is greatest, and at this moment the pressure of the person on the floor is smallest.
Therefore AD are correct.
**Answer:** $\rm AD$.
**Formula:** weightlessness $F_{\rm N}=m(g-a)< mg$.
