# Kinetic Energy Theorem

## Formula

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Kinetic energy: } &E_{\rm k}=\frac{1}{2}mv^2 \\
\text{Kinetic energy theorem: } &W=E_{\rm k2}-E_{\rm k1}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

## Example 1

(**2023 National Paper B, Q 2-21**) (Multiple choice) (6 points) As shown in the figure, a board of mass $M$ and length $l$ rests on a smooth horizontal table, and a small block of mass $m$ (which may be treated as a particle) starts moving with velocity $v_0$ from the left end of the board. The magnitude of the kinetic friction between the block and the board is known to be $f$. When the block leaves the board from its right end ($\qquad$).

![example1](./09 example1.html)

A. the kinetic energy of the board certainly equals $fl$
B. the kinetic energy of the board is certainly less than $fl$
C. the kinetic energy of the block is certainly greater than $\dfrac{1}{2}mv_0^2-fl$

D. the kinetic energy of the block is certainly less than $\dfrac{1}{2}mv_0^2-fl$

**Analysis:**
(1) For the board, by the kinetic energy theorem, $fx_M=\frac{1}{2}M{v_1'}^2-0$, and since $x_M < l$, the final kinetic energy of the board is certainly less than $fl$. A is wrong, B is correct.
(2) For the block, by the kinetic energy theorem, $-fx_m=\frac{1}{2}mv_{1}^2-\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2$, and since $x_m>l$, the final kinetic energy of the block is certainly less than $\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2-fl$. C is wrong, D is correct.
**Answer:** BD.
**Formula:** kinetic energy theorem $W=E_{k2}-E_{k1}$.

## Example 2

(**2023 National Paper B, Q 2-14**) (6 points) A student bumps a volleyball upward from point $O$; the volleyball moves vertically upward and then falls back to point $O$. Suppose the magnitude of the air resistance on the volleyball during its motion is proportional to its speed. Then the volleyball ($\qquad$).
A. takes the same time to rise as to fall
B. has its greatest speed at the instant just after being bumped up
C. has zero acceleration when it reaches the highest point
D. moves with uniform acceleration while falling
**Analysis:**
(1) The gravity is downward both when rising and when falling; when rising with decreasing speed it experiences a downward resistance ($mg+F_f=ma_1$), and when falling with increasing speed it experiences an upward resistance ($mg-F_f=ma_2$), so $a_1>a_2$, and the rising time is smaller than the falling time ($x=\frac{1}{2}at^2$). A is wrong.
(2) Over the whole process the work done by gravity is $W_G=0$, and the resistance always does negative work, $W_f=-2fh$; let the initial speed be $v_1$ and the final speed be $v_2$; by the kinetic energy theorem $W_G+W_f=\frac{1}{2}mv_2^2-\frac{1}{2}mv_1^2$, which gives $v_1>v_2$; also the speed decreases while rising and increases while falling, so the speed of the volleyball is greatest at the instant just after being bumped up. B is correct.
(3) By Newton's second law $F=ma$, and when the volleyball reaches the highest point the net force equals the gravity, so the acceleration then is $a=g$. C is wrong.
(4) While falling, the gravity is unchanged while the resistance increases, so the net force becomes smaller $F\downarrow$; by $F=ma$, the volleyball speeds up with decreasing acceleration. D is wrong.
**Answer:** B.
**Formulas:** displacement and time $x=\frac{1}{2}at^2$; Newton's second law $F=ma$; kinetic energy theorem $W=E_{k2}-E_{k1}$.

## Example 3

(**2024 National New Curriculum Paper, Q 2-15**) (6 points) The Fujian is the first catapult-type aircraft carrier designed and built independently by our country. Launching tests can be carried out with a counterweight trolley: during the test the counterweight trolley is catapulted horizontally off the deck by the catapult and then falls onto the sea. The catapult device is adjusted so that the kinetic energy of the trolley when it leaves the deck horizontally becomes $4$ times that before the adjustment. Neglecting air resistance, the horizontal distance between the landing point of the trolley on the sea and the point where it leaves the deck is ($\qquad$) that before the adjustment.

| A. $0.25$ times | B. $0.5$ times | C. $2$ times | D. $4$ times |
|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|

**Analysis:**
By the kinetic energy $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ and the projectile motion $x=v_0t$, when $E_k$ becomes $4$ times that before the adjustment the speed becomes $2$ times that before the adjustment, so the horizontal distance becomes $2$ times that before the adjustment.
**Answer:** $\rm C$.
**Formulas:** projectile motion $x=v_0t$; kinetic energy $E_k=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$.

## Example 4

(2024 National New Curriculum Paper, Q 3-24) (10 points) When heavy objects are transported from the window of a high building to the ground, for safety the object must keep a certain distance from the wall of the building while descending. As shown in the figure, a simple method is that one person high up controls the rope $P$ one of whose ends is tied to the object, and another person on the ground controls the other rope $Q$ one of whose ends is also tied to the object; the two persons cooperate so that the object descends vertically and slowly. If the mass of the object is $m=42\,\rm kg$, the magnitude of the gravitational acceleration is $g=10\,\rm m/s^2$, and when the angle between rope $P$ and the vertical is $\alpha=37^\circ$ the angle between rope $Q$ and the vertical is $\beta=53^\circ$ ($\sin 37^\circ =0.6$).

![example4-1](./09 example4-1.html)

(1) Find the magnitudes of the tensions in ropes $P$ and $Q$ at this moment;
(2) If the object is at a height $h=10\,\rm m$ above the ground when it starts to descend vertically, find the total work done by the tensions of the two ropes on the object during its descent to the ground.
**Analysis:**
(1)
Analyzing the forces on the object, as shown in the figure

![example4-2](./09 example4-2.html)

in the horizontal direction $T_P\sin\alpha=T_Q\sin\beta$, and in the vertical direction $T_P\cos\alpha=T_Q\cos\beta+mg$; solving them together gives
$$
\begin{cases}
T_P=1\,200\,{\rm N},\\
T_Q=900\,{\rm N}.
\end{cases}
$$
(2)
By the kinetic energy theorem, $mgh+W=0$, which gives $W=-4\,200\,\rm J$.
**Answer:** (1) $1\,200\,\rm N$, $900\,\rm N$; (2) $-4\,200\,\rm J$.
**Formula:** kinetic energy theorem $W=E_{k2}-E_{k1}$.

## Example 5

(**2023 National New Curriculum Paper, Q 2-15**) (6 points) When there is no wind, raindrops fall vertically at a constant speed near the ground under the action of air resistance. During the process in which a raindrop of mass $m$ falls a height $h$ at speed $v$ near the ground, the work done against the air resistance is (the magnitude of the gravitational acceleration is $g$) ($\qquad$).
A. $0$ B. $mgh$ C. $\frac{1}{2}mv^2-mgh$ D. $\frac{1}{2}mv^2+mgh$
**Analysis:**
By the kinetic energy theorem, $W_f=mgh$.
**Answer:** B.
**Formula:** kinetic energy theorem $W=E_{k2}-E_{k1}$.

## Example 6

(**2023 National New Curriculum Paper, Q 2-20**) (Multiple choice) (6 points) An object of mass $1\,\rm kg$ starts from rest on horizontal ground under the action of a horizontal pulling force and moves along the $x$ axis, with the starting point as the zero of the $x$ axis. The work $W$ done by the pulling force against the coordinate $x$ of the object is as shown in the figure. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the object and the horizontal ground is $0.4$, and the magnitude of the gravitational acceleration is taken as $10\,\rm m/s^2$. Which of the following statements is correct? ($\qquad$).

![example6](./09 example6.html)

A. At $x=1\,\rm m$ the power of the pulling force is $6\,\rm W$
B. At $x=4\,\rm m$ the kinetic energy of the object is $2\,\rm J$
C. From $x=0$ to $x=2\,\rm m$ the work done by the object against the friction is $8\,\rm J$
D. During the motion from $x=0$ to $x=4\,\rm m$ the maximum momentum of the object is $2\,\rm kg\cdot m/s$
**Analysis:**
From the work formula $F=\frac{W}{x}$, the slope of the graph represents the magnitude of the force, and the kinetic friction is $F_f=\mu F_{\rm N}=\mu mg=4\,\rm N$.
(1) During $0\sim 1\,\rm m$ the work done by the net force is $W_1-F_fx_1$; by the kinetic energy theorem $W_1-F_fx_1=\frac{1}{2}mv_1^2-0$, which gives $v_1=2\,\rm m/s$; in this process (the graph is a straight line within $0\sim 2\,\rm m$, and its slope is the magnitude of the pulling force) $F_1=\frac{12\,\rm J}{2\,\rm m}=6\,\rm N$, so the power of the pulling force is $P=F_1v_1=12\,\rm W$. A is wrong.
(2) During $0\sim 4\,\rm m$, by the kinetic energy theorem $W_4-F_fx_4=E_{\rm k4}-0$, which gives $E_{\rm k4}=2\,\rm J$. B is correct.
(3) During $0\sim 2\,\rm m$ the work done by the object against the friction is $W_f=F_f x_2=8\,\rm J$. C is correct.
(4) During $0\sim 4\,\rm m$ the speed and the momentum of the object are greatest at $x=2\,\rm m$; by the kinetic energy theorem $W_2-F_fx_2=\frac{1}{2}mv^2_2$, which gives $v_2=2\sqrt{2}\,\rm m/s$, so the maximum momentum is $p_m=mv_2=2\sqrt{2}\,\rm kg\cdot m/s$. D is wrong.
**Answer:** $\rm BC$.
**Formulas:** kinetic friction $F_f=\mu F_{\rm N}$; work $W=Fl\cos\theta$; power $P=Fv$; kinetic energy theorem $W=E_{k2}-E_{k1}$.
