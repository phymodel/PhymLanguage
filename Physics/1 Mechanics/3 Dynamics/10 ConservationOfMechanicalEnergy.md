## Example 2

(2023 National Paper A, Q 2-14) (6 points) A student pushes a shot horizontally; neglecting air resistance and the effect of rotation, during the projectile motion of the shot ($\qquad$).

| A. its mechanical energy keeps increasing | B. its acceleration stays unchanged | C. the magnitude of its velocity stays unchanged | D. its kinetic energy is greatest at the instant after being pushed |
|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|

**Analysis:**
(1) In projectile motion only gravity acts, so the mechanical energy is conserved ($\Delta E_k=-\Delta E_p$); A is wrong.
(2) The acceleration of the shot is the gravitational acceleration, which stays unchanged; B is correct.
(3) Gravity always does positive work, so the speed keeps increasing; C is wrong.
(4) Gravity always does positive work, so the kinetic energy keeps increasing; D is wrong.
**Answer:** $\rm B$.
**Formula:** conservation of mechanical energy $\Delta E_k=-\Delta E_p$.

## Example 3

(2023 National Paper A, Q 3-24) (12 points) As shown in the figure, a light spring is placed on a smooth horizontal table, with one end fixed to a wall. A small ball of mass $m$ presses the other end of the spring, giving the spring an elastic potential energy $E_p$. After being released, the ball starts from rest and moves on the table under the action of the spring, and leaves the table horizontally after separating from the spring. At the instant after the ball collides with the horizontal ground, the component of its velocity parallel to the ground equals that at the instant before the collision, and the magnitude of the component perpendicular to the ground becomes $\dfrac{4}{5}$ of that at the instant before the collision. The maximum height the ball bounces to after colliding with the ground is $h$. The magnitude of the gravitational acceleration is $g$, and air resistance is neglected. Find

![example3](./10 example3.html)

(1) the magnitude of the velocity of the ball when it leaves the table;
(2) the horizontal distance between the point where the ball first lands and the point where it leaves the table.
**Analysis:**
(1) By the law of conservation of mechanical energy $\Delta E_k=-\Delta E_p$, $E_p=\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2$, which gives $v_0=\sqrt{\frac{2E_p}{m}}$.
(2) From the knowledge of projectile motion and the conditions of the problem,
$$
\begin{cases}
x=v_0t,\\
v_y=gt,\\
v_{y'}=\frac{4}{5}v_y,\\
v_{y'}^2-0=2gh,
\end{cases}
$$
which gives
$$
x=\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{\frac{hE_p}{mg}}.
$$
**Answer:** (1) $\sqrt{\frac{2E_p}{m}}$; (2) $\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{\frac{hE_p}{mg}}$.
**Formulas:** projectile motion $x=v_0t,\,v_y=gt$; law of conservation of mechanical energy $\Delta E_k=-\Delta E_p$.

## Example 4

(2022 National Paper A, Q 2-14) (6 points) The figure shows a partial schematic diagram of the Big Air Shougang ski jump of the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics. An athlete slides down freely from rest at $a$ and takes off at $b$; point $c$ is the lowest point between $a$ and $b$, and the height difference between $a$ and $c$ is $h$. It is required that the pressure of the athlete on the skis when passing point $c$ be no greater than $k$ times the gravity acting on the athlete. The athlete is treated as a particle and all resistance is neglected during the motion. Then the radius of this circular arc section of the snow track at point $c$ should not be smaller than ($\qquad$).

![example4](./10 example4.html)

| A. $\frac{h}{k+1}$ | B. $\frac{h}{k}$ | C. $\frac{2h}{k}$ | D. $\frac{2h}{k-1}$ |
|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|:-----:|

**Analysis:**
Let the speed at point $c$ be $v_c$ and the mass of the athlete be $m$. According to the problem, when the athlete reaches point $c$:
by the law of conservation of mechanical energy, $mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv_c^2$;
by Newton's second law, $F_N-mg=\frac{mv_c^2}{r}$;
by Newton's third law, $F_N\leq kmg$;
combining these gives $r\geq \frac{2h}{k-1}$.
Therefore D is correct.
**Answer:** $\rm D$.
**Formulas:** $F=ma$; $-\Delta E_p=\Delta E_k$.
