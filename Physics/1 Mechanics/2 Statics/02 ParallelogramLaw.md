# Parallelogram Law

## Formula

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Resultant force: } &F=\sqrt{F_1^2+F_2^2+2F_1F_2\cos\theta}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

## Concurrent Forces

- Definition: if several forces all act at the same point of a body, or if their lines of action intersect at one point, these forces are called concurrent forces.
- Several forces act at the same point at the same time.
- The extensions of the lines of action of several forces can intersect at one point.
- Several forces acting on a body that is treated as a point mass may be regarded as concurrent forces.

## Resultant and Components

- Resultant force: if a single force acting alone produces the same effect as several forces acting together, this single force is called the resultant of those forces.
- Components: if several forces acting together produce the same effect as one single force acting alone, these forces are called the components of that single force.
- Relation between the resultant and the components: it is a relation of equivalent substitution — the effect of the resultant is the same as the combined effect of the components.
- Magnitude and direction: once the magnitudes and directions of the two components are fixed, the resultant is also fixed.
- Range of the magnitude of the resultant: $|F_1-F_2|\leqslant F\leqslant F_1+F_2$.

## Composition and Resolution of Forces

- Composition of forces: the process of finding the resultant of several forces.
- Resolution of forces: the process of finding the components of one force. (Given the resultant and the magnitude and direction of one component; given the resultant and the directions of both components.)
- Parallelogram law: draw a parallelogram with the directed segments of the two forces as adjacent sides; the diagonal represents the magnitude and direction of the resultant.
- Parallelogram law formula (law of cosines): $F=\sqrt{F_1^2+F_2^2+2F_1F_2\cos\theta}$.

## Vectors and Scalars

- Vector: a physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction and whose addition obeys the parallelogram law.
- Scalar: a physical quantity that has magnitude but no direction and whose addition obeys the rules of arithmetic.

## Equilibrium of Concurrent Forces

- Condition for equilibrium under concurrent forces: under the action of concurrent forces, a body is in equilibrium when the resultant force is zero.
- Equilibrium of a body: when a body is acted on by several forces and remains at rest or in uniform rectilinear motion, we say the body is in a state of equilibrium.

## Experiment: Investigating the Composition of Two Forces at an Angle

## Exam Question 1

(**2023 National Paper B, Questions 3-22**) (5 points) The apparatus used in the experiment "Verifying the parallelogram law of forces" is: a wooden board, white paper, two standard spring dynamometers, a rubber band, a light small ring, a ruler, a pencil, thin threads and several drawing pins. Complete the following experimental steps:
① Fix the white paper to the horizontal wooden board with drawing pins.
② Fix one end of the rubber band to the board and tie the other end to the light small ring. Tie the two thin threads to the small ring as well, and hang a dynamometer on the free end of each thread. Pull the two dynamometers with forces of suitable magnitude, at a certain angle to each other and parallel to the board; when the small ring comes to rest, obtain the magnitudes of the two pulling forces $F_1$ and $F_2$ from the readings of the two dynamometers, and $\underline{\qquad\qquad}$. (Multiple choice; fill in the letters of the correct options.)
A. Measure the length of the rubber band with the ruler
B. Measure the lengths of the two thin threads with the ruler
C. Mark the position of the small ring on the white paper with the pencil
D. Mark the directions of the two thin threads on the white paper with the pencil
③ Remove one dynamometer and pull the small ring with the other dynamometer to $\underline{\qquad\qquad}$; obtain the magnitude of the pulling force $F$ from the dynamometer reading, and mark the direction of $F$ at this moment along the thin thread.
④ Choose a suitable scale, and from the results of step ② draw on the white paper, according to the parallelogram law, the composition diagram of $F_1$ and $F_2$ to obtain the magnitude and direction of the resultant $F'$; then draw the force diagram of $F$ on the white paper using the same scale.
⑤ Compare the $\underline{\qquad\qquad}$ of $F'$ and $F$, so as to judge whether this experiment verifies the parallelogram law of forces.
**Analysis:**
② There is no need to measure the lengths of the rubber band and the thin threads, so $\rm A$ and $\rm B$ are wrong; the resultant and the components are related by equivalent substitution, so the position of the small ring and the magnitude and direction of each force must be marked — the magnitudes are read from the dynamometers and the directions are those of the thin threads, so $\rm C$ and $\rm D$ are correct.
③ Remove one dynamometer and pull the small ring with the other dynamometer to the position of the small ring marked on the white paper; obtain the magnitude of the pulling force $F$ from the dynamometer reading, and mark the direction of $F$ at this moment along the thin thread;
⑤ Compare whether the magnitudes and directions of $F'$ and $F$ agree within the allowed experimental error, so as to judge whether this experiment verifies the parallelogram law of forces.
**Answer:** ② $\rm CD$; ③ to the marked position; ⑤ magnitude and direction.
**Formula:** resultant $F=\sqrt{F_1^2+F_2^2+2F_1F_2\cos\theta}$.

## Exam Question 2

(**2019 National Paper I, Questions 2-19**) (Multiple choice) (6 points) As shown in the figure, a rough inclined plane is fixed on the ground, and a smooth fixed pulley is mounted at the top of the plane. A thin string passes over the pulley; one end of it hangs a block $N$, and the other end is connected to a block $M$ on the plane, and the system is at rest. A horizontal pulling force to the left is now applied to pull $N$ slowly, until the string hanging $N$ makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the vertical. It is known that $M$ remains at rest throughout. Then during this process ($\qquad$).
A. The magnitude of the horizontal pulling force may stay constant
B. The magnitude of the tension in the string acting on $M$ must keep increasing
C. The magnitude of the friction acting on $M$ from the plane must keep increasing
D. The magnitude of the friction acting on $M$ from the plane may first decrease and then increase
【Answer】$\rm BD$.
【Analysis】
As shown in the figure,
(1) The tension in the string gradually increases; A is wrong.
(2) As the tension in the string gradually increases, the magnitude of the tension acting on $M$ must keep increasing; B is correct.
(3) If the friction is directed down the plane before the pull, then as the tension gradually increases the friction $F_f$ also gradually increases; if the friction is directed up the plane before the pull, then as the tension gradually increases the friction $F_f$ may first decrease and then increase; C is wrong, D is correct.

## Exam Question 3

(**2017 National Paper I, Questions 2-21**) (Multiple choice) (6 points) As shown in the figure, the end $O$ of a soft light string $ON$ is fixed, a weight is tied to some point $M$ in the middle of the string, and the other end $N$ of the string is held by hand. Initially $OM$ is vertical and $MN$ is taut, and the angle between $OM$ and $MN$ is $\alpha\left(\alpha>\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$. The weight is now slowly pulled up to the right while the angle $\alpha$ is kept unchanged. During the process in which $OM$ is pulled from vertical to horizontal ($\qquad$).
A. The tension in $MN$ gradually increases
B. The tension in $MN$ first increases and then decreases
C. The tension in $OM$ gradually increases
D. The tension in $OM$ first increases and then decreases
【Answer】$\rm AD$.
【Analysis】
As shown in the figure,
where $\alpha$ is always greater than $90^\circ$ and constant, $\beta$ gradually increases from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$, and $\alpha-\beta$ gradually decreases from more than $90^\circ$ to less than $90^\circ$.
(1) Since $\dfrac{G}{\sin(180^\circ-\alpha)}=\dfrac{F_{MN}}{\sin\beta}$, we have $F_{MN}=\dfrac{G\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha}$, so $F_{MN}$ gradually increases; hence A is correct and B is wrong.
(2) Since $\dfrac{G}{\sin(180^\circ-\alpha)}=\dfrac{F_{MO}}{\sin(\alpha-\beta)}$, we have $F_{MO}=\dfrac{G\sin(\alpha-\beta)}{\sin\alpha}$, so $F_{MO}$ first increases and then decreases; hence C is wrong and D is correct.
