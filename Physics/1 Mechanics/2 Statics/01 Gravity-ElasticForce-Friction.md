# Gravity, Elastic Force and Friction

## Formula

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Gravity: } &G=mg \\
\text{Elastic force: } &F=kx \\
\text{Kinetic friction: } &F_f=\mu F_N
\end{aligned}
}
$$

## Gravity
- Definition: the force acting on a body because of the Earth's attraction. (A force is an interaction.)
- Formula: $G=mg$. ($g$ is the gravitational acceleration.)
- Direction: vertically downward.
- Unit: the newton, abbreviated N, symbol $\rm N$.
- Center of gravity: every part of a body is acted on by gravity; in terms of effect, the gravity acting on all the parts may be regarded as acting at a single point, and this point is called the center of gravity of the body.
- Factors that determine the center of gravity: the shape of the body and its mass distribution. (For a body of regular shape and uniform mass distribution, the center of gravity lies at its geometric center.)
- Force diagram: the length of a directed line segment represents the magnitude of the force, the arrowhead gives its direction, and the tail (or the head) gives its point of application. (Magnitude, direction, point of application.)
- Free-body sketch: an arrowed line segment shows the point of application and the direction of the force; the magnitude need not be drawn to an accurate scale.

## Elastic Force

- Elastic force: a deformed body that tends to return to its original shape exerts a force on the bodies in contact with it. (Deformation: under the action of a force, the shape or the volume of a body changes.)
- Direction of the elastic force: surface to surface (perpendicular to the common contact surface); point to surface (through the point, perpendicular to the surface); point to point (through the point, perpendicular to the tangent plane); light string (along the string, in the direction of contraction).

## Proportionality Law for the Deformation of an Elastic Body

- Definition: within the elastic limit, when a spring undergoes elastic deformation, the magnitude of the elastic force $F$ is directly proportional to the extension (or compression) $x$ of the spring.
Formula: $F=kx$. (Stiffness coefficient: $k$.)
- Unit of $k$: newton per metre, symbol $\rm N/m$.
Elastic limit: if the deformation is too large and exceeds a certain limit, the body cannot completely recover its original shape after the applied force is removed; such a limit is called the elastic limit.
Elastic deformation: if a body can recover its original shape after the applied force is removed, the deformation is called elastic deformation.
> Hooke's law is the formula for the elastic force of a spring; it is not a general formula for all elastic forces.

## Kinetic Friction

- Definition: when two bodies in contact slide relative to each other, a force that hinders the relative motion is produced on the contact surface.
- Conditions: an elastic force exists between the bodies, the contact surfaces are rough, and there is relative motion.
- Direction: always along the contact surface, and opposite to the direction of the relative motion of the body.
- Magnitude: the magnitude of kinetic friction is directly proportional to the normal force (support force) $F_N$.
- Formula: $F_f=\mu F_N$ (coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu$).
- Magnitude of $\mu$: it depends on the materials of the contact surfaces and on their roughness.

## Static Friction

- Definition: when two bodies in contact have only a tendency to move relative to each other but no relative motion, the friction between them is called static friction.
- Conditions: an elastic force exists between the bodies, the contact surfaces are rough, and there is a tendency to relative motion.
- Direction: always along the contact surface, and opposite to the direction of the tendency of relative motion.
- Maximum static friction: static friction has a maximum value $F_{\rm max}$, numerically equal to the external force along the direction of the tendency of relative motion that is needed to balance it just as relative motion is about to begin.
- Magnitude of static friction: the static friction $F$ that actually occurs between the two bodies lies between $0$ and $F_{\rm max}$, that is, $0<F\leqslant F_{\rm max}$.
