# Thin Lens Refraction

## Formula

$$
\boxed{
\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v}=\frac{1}{f},\quad \frac{1}{f}=(n-1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1}-\frac{1}{R_2}\right)
}
$$

## Thin Lens Formula (two spherical surfaces chained together)

Chaining the refraction formulas of the front and rear spherical surfaces and then letting the lens thickness $d\to0$ (thin-lens approximation) to eliminate the intermediate image distance gives:
$$\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v}=\frac{1}{f},\quad \frac{1}{f}=(n-1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1}-\frac{1}{R_2}\right)$$
This is the mathematical essence of what is called the "convex lens imaging rule" in middle school. **$1/u+1/v=1/f$ is a corollary of Snell's law under the thin-lens + paraxial conditions, not an independent principle.**
