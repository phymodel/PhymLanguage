# Snell's Law (Law of Refraction)

## Formula

$$
\boxed{
n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2
}
$$

## Symbol Explanation

- $n_1$: refractive index of medium 1
- $n_2$: refractive index of medium 2
- $\theta_1$: angle of incidence
- $\theta_2$: angle of refraction

- $v$: speed of light in the medium
- $c$: speed of light in vacuum

## Qualitative Rules

- Air → water (optically thinner → optically denser): $\theta_2<\theta_1$, the ray bends toward the normal
- Water → air (optically denser → optically thinner): $\theta_2>\theta_1$, the ray bends away from the normal
- Normal incidence: $\theta_1=0 \Rightarrow \theta_2=0$, no deviation
- The light path is reversible

## Definition of the Refractive Index

Define the refractive index as
$$
n=\frac{c}{v}
$$

## Derivation of Snell's Law

From the continuity of the wavefronts:

$$
\frac{\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2} = \frac{v_1}{v_2}
$$

Using the refractive index:

$$
n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2
$$

## Critical Angle and Total Internal Reflection

When light travels from an optically denser medium 1 into an optically thinner medium 2 (that is, $n_1 > n_2$), the angle of refraction $\theta_2$ increases as the angle of incidence $\theta_1$ increases;
the angle of incidence corresponding to $\theta_2 = 90^\circ$ is called the **critical angle** $\theta_c$:

$$
\sin\theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1}
$$

If $\theta_1 > \theta_c$, there is no refracted ray and **total internal reflection** occurs (all the energy returns into medium 1).
Conversely, when $n_1 \le n_2$, no critical angle exists.
